Why Juniper

The name comes from the Utah juniper, Juniperus osteosperma. She has a unique way of life, compared to the generic pine trees in the valley below.

Down in the valley, the pines seem to have it easy. They have more water and softer soil. They grow close together, and each one takes the same shape as the tree beside it. From a distance, it looks like belonging. Up close, it looks like confinement.

Most of us get conditioned to be like a valley pine.

You must follow the rules of the group, you must do what you’re told and you must like it, or you’re weird and a failure. Yet, the rules of the group are often ridiculous, confusing and exhausting. Unlike a valley pine that can’t uproot itself and choose different ground and shape, any human in a first-world nation can.

And as Juniper proves, choosing different grounding (conditions) and shape can lead to thriving.

She lives on open rock, exposed to wind and drought, with no crowd around. She learned to read the ground and specialize her roots to reach deep and wide to find water other trees would never find. When drought comes, she can let one branch go quiet so the rest of her keeps living. Her trunk twists, her branches spread low, and she can stand strong on the same stone for hundreds of years.

None of her shape was chosen to fit in or stand out.

It’s simply how she has adapted to the unique circumstances of her life. And you, like the Juniper, can choose a more effective way to find flow and thrive, whenever you’re ready to believe that it’s possible.
